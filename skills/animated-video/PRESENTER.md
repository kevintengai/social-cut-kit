# Presenter

When A Filmed Presenter Appears In An Animated Film, Decide Where They Stand In It Before Anything Is Drawn Or Filmed. A Presenter Added Late Ends Up Pasted On.

## Three Ways A Presenter Can Appear

| Way | What It Is | What It Costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean Cuts | Full-Frame Presenter Shots Alternate With Full-Frame Animation. | Nothing To Match. It Never Looks Fake. |
| Animation Around The Presenter | Graphics Sit In The Filmed Frame Beside, Above, Or In Front Of The Presenter. | Room In The Frame, Planned Before Filming. No Cut-Out Is Needed When Graphics Stay Off The Body. |
| Presenter Inside An Animated World | The Presenter Is Cut From Their Background And Placed Over Animation. | A Clean Matte, And Every Match Listed Below |

The Kit's Timeline Places Animation Over Footage Or Between Shots, As Described In [Handoffs](HANDOFFS.md). A Replaced Background Needs A Matte Made In The Owner's Editor. Choose The Simplest Way That Serves The Film. Clean Cuts Are Always An Option.

## Design The Presenter's Surroundings First

Do This In The Beat Sheet From [Directing](DIRECTING.md), Not After The Shoot.

- For Each Presenter Beat, Write Where They Stand In The Frame, What Is Behind Them, What Appears Beside Them, On Which Side, And How Large.
- Leave Empty Space On The Side Where Graphics Will Appear. A Presenter Framed Dead Center Leaves Nowhere To Put Anything.
- Note Where The Presenter Looks And Gestures. A Hand That Points At The Spot Where An Object Will Appear Ties The Two Worlds Together.
- When The Footage Already Exists, Design The Animation Around What Was Shot. The Free Space, The Light, And The Lens Are Now Fixed.

## Plan The Frame Before Filming

- **Lock The Camera.** Use A Tripod At Eye Height. Graphics Hold Still, So A Handheld Frame Makes Them Appear To Float.
- **Lock Exposure And White Balance.** Automatic Changes During A Take Cannot Be Matched Afterward.
- **Light Simply.** One Soft Key From One Side, The Same Side The Animation Will Be Lit From. Avoid Mixing Daylight And Lamp Light.
- **Choose The Background.** A Plain, Evenly Lit Wall That Contrasts With The Hair Helps Any Cut-Out. A Real Location Is Better When The Background Will Stay.
- **Frame For The Delivery.** Shoot At The Film's Frame Rate And At A Higher Resolution Than The Delivery Where Possible. Frame To The Final Size In Camera.
- **Dress For It.** Avoid Colors Close To The Background, Fine Patterns That Shimmer, And Any Color The Film Has Given A Meaning.
- **Mark The Graphics.** Put A Small Marker Where Each Object Will Appear, In A Spot The Graphic Will Cover, So The Eyeline And The Gesture Are True.
- **Record Extras.** Capture A Few Seconds Of The Empty Background, And Have The Presenter Hold Still At The Head And Tail Of Each Take.
- **Record Clean Sound.** The Presenter's Voice Is Kept As Recorded By Default. Processing Is Opt-In.

## What Makes A Composite Believable

- **Only What That Camera Could See.** The Added World Obeys The Camera That Filmed The Presenter. The Horizon Sits At The Camera's Height. Scale Follows Distance. Background Blur Matches The Lens. Things In Front Cover The Presenter And Things Behind Are Covered. If The Camera Moved, The Added Elements Move With It. A World That Is Openly Graphic Is Safer Than A Realistic Room That Is Slightly Wrong.
- **Light That Agrees.** The Light On The Background Comes From The Same Side As The Light On The Face, With The Same Softness And The Same Warmth. Where The Presenter Touches A Surface, Add Their Shadow, Or Frame From The Waist Up So No Contact Shows. A Faint Tint From The Animated World On The Presenter's Edges Helps Them Sit In It.
- **Matched Black Level.** Sample The Darkest Areas Of The Footage And Of The Animation On A Still. Phone Footage Has Raised, Noisy Shadows. Pure Black Beside It Looks Pasted. Lift The Animation's Darkest Tones To Meet The Footage.
- **Matched Contrast.** Phone Footage Arrives Sharpened And Tone-Mapped. Clean Animation Beside It Looks Like A Different Medium. Bring Contrast And Saturation Toward Each Other.
- **Matched Grain.** Add Noise To The Animated Elements At The Size And Strength Of The Footage's Noise, Changing On Every Frame As It Does In The Footage.
- **Matched Sharpness.** Animated Edges Are Sharper Than Any Camera Resolves. Soften Them By A Fraction Of A Pixel Until They Sit With The Footage, And Blur Moving Elements As The Camera Would.
- **Careful Edges On Hair.** Hair Is Where Composites Fail. A Hard Matte Turns Hair Into A Helmet. A Soft One Leaves A Halo Of The Old Background. Keep The Original Background Behind The Hair Wherever Possible, By Placing Graphics Beside The Presenter. When The Background Must Change, Choose A New One Close In Brightness And Color To The Old One Near The Head. Replacing A Bright Background With A Dark One Behind Fine Hair Is The Hardest Case. Inspect Edges At Full Size On Stills, And In Strips Of Consecutive Frames, Where A Crawling Edge Shows.
- **No Big Digital Push-Ins On Phone Footage.** Phone Footage Is Already Sharpened And Compressed. Enlarging It Much Beyond Its Native Framing Shows Mush, Noise, And Halos, And Leaves The Presenter Softer Than The Crisp Animation Around Them. That Mismatch Gives The Composite Away. Get The Framing In Camera, Or Film A Second, Closer Take.

## Trim On-Camera Shots To Eyes On The Lens

Start Each Presenter Shot On A Frame Where The Eyes Are Already On The Lens. End It Before They Glance Away To A Script, A Monitor, Or The Stop Button. A Glance Off The Lens Reads As Reading, And It Breaks The Address To The Viewer.

- Check The First And Last Frame Of Every Presenter Shot On Stills, With A Short Strip Around Each.
- Avoid Cutting In The Middle Of A Blink.
- Keep Words Whole. When A Glance Overlaps Speech, Keep The Words And Cover The Glance With Full-Frame Animation While The Voice Continues.

## Test One Finished Shot First

1. Choose The Hardest Shot. That Is The One With The Finest Hair, The Most Movement, Or The Largest Change Of Background.
2. Finish It Completely At Delivery Size, With The Matte, The Light Match, The Black Level, The Grain, The Sharpness, And The Animation Around It.
3. Inspect Stills At Full Size And At Phone Size, And A Strip For Crawling Edges.
4. Show The Owner The Stills And The Editable Project. No Video File Is Created Before The Owner Reviews The Editable Project In Remotion Studio And Approves, Including A Test Or Preview Export.
5. Record The Recipe That Worked, With The Black Lift, The Grain Amount, The Softening, And The Tint. Apply It To The Remaining Shots.

If The Hardest Shot Cannot Be Made Believable, The Others Will Not Rescue It. Fall Back To Clean Cuts, Or To Animation Around The Presenter.

## Clean Cuts Are Always An Option

Cutting Between A Full-Frame Presenter And Full-Frame Animation Needs No Matte And No Matching.

- **Share Color.** Bring The Film's Palette Into The Presenter's Background Or Clothing. A Gentle Grade On The Footage Is A Second Route, With The Owner's Agreement.
- **Share Sound.** Let The Presenter's Voice Continue Over The Animation. On Screen They Are The Host. Off Screen They Are The Narrator.
- **Share Motion.** Cut On A Gesture. The Hand Points And The Next Frame Is The Thing Pointed At.
- **Use Each For What It Does Best.** A Face Is Right For The Opening, A Personal Claim, And The Ask. Animation Is Right For The Explanation.
- **Consider An Honest Inset.** A Presenter In A Framed Window Over The Animation Is A Designed Layout, Not A Faked Space, And It Needs No Matching.

Report Any Replaced Background Or Other Synthetic Element As Described In [Review](REVIEW.md).
