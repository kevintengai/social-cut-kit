# Style Frames

Choose The Look After The Plan From [Directing](DIRECTING.md) Is Approved. For A Short Film, The Look May Be Shown In The Same Message As The Plan, As The Approval Stop In That Guide Describes, And Each Still Needs Its Own Explicit Yes. The Owner Picks By Looking At A Beat Rendered In Each Offered Style. A Description Of A Style Is Not A Choice Anyone Can Make.

A Style Frame Needs The Film Folder, The Script, And The Word Timings From The Earlier Steps, And One Scene Module For The Chosen Beat. It Is The Only Finished-Looking Picture Made Before The Look Is Approved.

## The Styles

| Style | Suits | Signature | Typical Failure | Guide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hand-Made 2D | Stories, Teaching, Personal Brands, Anything That Should Feel Made By A Person | A Line That Varies, Paper And Pigment Texture, Drawings Held For Two Or Three Frames | A Wobble Filter On Clean Vector Shapes | [Hand-Made 2D](styles/handmade-2d.md) |
| Editorial Collage | History, Culture, Opinion, Business Analysis, Subjects With Real Photos And Documents | Cut-Out Photos, Paper Edges, Bold Type Blocks, A Small Palette, Stepped Movement | A Scrapbook With No Grid And Mixed Treatments | [Editorial Collage](styles/editorial-collage.md) |
| Flat Infographic, Kinetic Type, And Product-Interface Films | Data, Processes, Software, Announcements | Clean Shapes On A Grid, Precise Easing, Type Used As Image, An Interface Redrawn Simply | A Template Look Where Everything Eases The Same Way | [Motion Graphics](styles/motion-graphics.md) |
| Luminous Dark 3D | Networks, Data, Energy, Finance, Systems Nobody Can Photograph | A Dark Stage, Glowing Lines And Points, Depth Haze, A Slow Camera | Glow On Everything And No Focal Point | [Luminous 3D](styles/luminous-3d.md) |
| Realistic 3D | Physical Products The Viewer Must Believe Exist | True Scale, Real Materials, Studio Light, Motion With Weight | Code-Drawn Objects Presented As Photographs | [Realistic 3D](styles/realistic-3d.md) |

## Choose From The Subject And The Register

Start With What The Subject Really Has, Then Fit The Register The Owner Wants.

- **Real Material.** Photos, Documents, And Archive Point To Collage. A Physical Product Points To Realistic 3D. Software Points To A Product-Interface Film. Figures Point To Infographic. A System Nobody Can See Points To Luminous 3D Or Infographic. People And Feelings Point To Characters Or Hand-Made 2D. Places Point To A Map.
- **Register.** Decide Whether The Film Should Feel Authoritative, Warm, Playful, Calm, Or Energetic. The Same Subject Takes A Different Style At A Different Register. A Bank Explaining Fees Can Be A Sober Infographic Or A Friendly Doodle, And Those Are Different Films.
- **Brand.** The Style Has To Carry The Owner's Existing Colors And Type Without Strain.
- **Screen.** The Style Has To Stay Legible On A Phone At The Delivery Aspect Ratio.
- **Other Commitments.** A Filmed [Presenter](PRESENTER.md), The Length, And The Build Cost All Narrow The Field.

Offer Two Or Three Styles That Could Each Do The Job. Do Not Choose By What The Kit Makes Easiest Or By What Was Made Last Time.

## How Many Frames To Show

The Owner's Stated Preference Decides How Many Frames Are Shown. It Comes Before The Kit's Default.

- **No Preference Stated.** Render The Same Beat In Two Or Three Styles, Side By Side.
- **The Owner Asks For One Style Frame.** Render The One Style You Recommend, With The Reason. Name The Alternatives In A Single Line, Each With What It Would Change, And Offer To Render Them. Do Not Render Frames They Did Not Ask For.
- **The Owner Names A Style.** Render That Style. Mention One Alternative Only When The Named Style Has A Clear Problem For This Subject, And Say What The Problem Is.
- **The Owner Asks For More.** Render More, Up To What You Can Sustain Across The Whole Film.

One Frame Or Three, The Look Is Approved Only By An Explicit Yes To A Picture The Owner Has Seen.

## Render The Same Beat In Each Offered Style

1. Choose The Beat That Carries The Film. Prefer One That Contains The Hardest Elements, Such As A Character, A Line Of Text, And The Key Object.
2. Render That Beat In Each Style You Are Showing, With The Same Words And The Same Intent, At The Delivery Aspect Ratio. Use `tools/stills.cjs` As Described In [Review Tools](REVIEW-TOOLS.md), And Write The Stills Into The Film Folder In The User's Project.
3. Finish Each Frame Far Enough To Judge. Use The Real Colors, The Real Type, The Texture, And The Light. A Style Frame Is A Promise. Show Only What You Can Sustain Across The Whole Film.
4. Where A Style Depends On Movement, Add A Second Still Or A Short Frame Strip, And A Line On How It Moves. The Held Drawings Of Hand-Made 2D Cannot Be Seen In One Still.
5. When More Than One Frame Is Shown, Put Them Side By Side On One Board With Plain Labels. State Your Recommendation And The Reason Either Way.
6. When The Owner Asks For A Blend, Such As The Texture Of One With The Palette Of Another, Render The Blend Before Building. Blends Fail In Ways A Description Hides.

Stills Are Not Video Files. Nothing Is Exported At This Stage.

After The Choice, Write A Short Style Sheet In The Project Notes. Record The Palette With The Meaning Of Each Color, The Type, The Line, The Texture, The Frame Rate, The Light, And The Camera Rules. Every Scene Follows It.

## A Liked Earlier Film Is Evidence Of Taste, Not A Default

When The Owner Says They Liked An Earlier Film, Find Out What They Liked. It May Have Been The Pace, The Humor, The Clarity, The Palette, Or The Look Itself. Ask, Or State Your Reading And Have It Confirmed.

- What They Liked Tells You About Their Taste. It Does Not Decide The Style Of A New Film About A Different Subject.
- Offer The Liked Look As One Candidate When It Suits The New Subject, Beside At Least One Alternative Chosen For This Brief. Say Which You Recommend.
- Do Not Lift The Earlier Film's Scenes, Parts, And Palette As A Template. Reuse A Part Only Where It Is The Right Picture.
- An Explicit Instruction To Match The Earlier Film Is Different. Follow It, And Still Render The Key Beat First To Confirm The Look Holds For The New Subject.

## Cost And Consistency

Weigh Drawing In Code Against Generated Footage Before Offering A Style. The Kit Draws In Code. Generated Footage Comes From A Separate Image Or Video Model That The Owner Chooses To Use.

| | Drawing In Code | Generated Footage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consistency | Every Frame Comes From One Description, So Characters, Colors, And Layout Stay Identical Across Shots. | Faces, Proportions, And Product Details Drift From Shot To Shot. |
| Timing | Events Land On Exact Frames Taken From The Voice. | Exact Timing To Words Is Hard To Obtain. |
| Text And Data | Exact | Unreliable |
| Revision | Local And Cheap. Change A Value And Render The Stills Again. | A New Generation Gives A Different Result. |
| Richness | Organic And Photographic Looks Are Costly. The Result Reads As Designed. | Rich Texture And Realism Arrive Quickly. |
| Disclosure | Nothing To Disclose | Synthetic Material Is Reported To The Owner. See [Review](REVIEW.md). |

- Draw In Code Whatever Must Stay Consistent, Carry Text Or Data, Or Land On A Word.
- Keep Generated Material For Atmosphere, Backgrounds, And Textures Where Drift Does Not Matter, And Only With The Owner's Agreement.
- Route A Product That Must Look Real To The Blender Scene Builder. See [Realistic 3D](styles/realistic-3d.md).
- Cost Shows Across The Whole Film, Not In One Frame. A Style That Looks Fine In A Single Still Can Need Many Times The Work Per Scene. Estimate The Cost Per Scene Before You Offer It.
