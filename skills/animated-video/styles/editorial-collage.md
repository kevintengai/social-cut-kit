# Editorial Collage

Real Fragments, Such As Photos, Documents, Maps, And Product Shots, Are Cut Out And Arranged With Bold Type And Flat Color On Paper, Like A Magazine Spread That Moves. Choose This Look In [Style Frames](../STYLE-FRAMES.md). The Drawing Helpers Are Described In [Code Scenes](../CODE-SCENES.md).

## What Makes It Read As Collage

- **Real Material.** The Images Come From The Subject's World. The Style Borrows The Authority Of A Photograph Or A Document.
- **Cut Edges And Layers.** Pieces Look Cut By Hand And Laid On Top Of Each Other, With Short Shadows Between Them.
- **A Grid And A Small Palette.** Strong Type, Blocks Of Flat Color, And Photos Reduced To One Shared Treatment.
- **Placed Movement.** Pieces Arrive As If A Hand Set Them Down. They Do Not Float.

## Material And Rights

- Use The Owner's Own Photos, Product Shots, Documents, And Captures First. Add Licensed Or Public-Domain Images Only With Their Source And Terms Recorded In The [Asset Library](../../asset-library/SKILL.md).
- Work From Files The Owner Supplies. Images Lifted From Other People's Posts Are Not Material.
- A Recognizable Person Needs Permission, And Must Not Appear To Endorse Something They Did Not.
- Keep A List Of Every Sourced Image. The Final Report In [Review](../REVIEW.md) Asks For It.
- The Image Must Be What The Words Say It Is. A Photo Of A Different Building Under The Words Our Workshop Is A False Statement.

## Cut-Outs

- Mask Each Subject With A Path Of Short, Slightly Irregular Segments. A Mask That Follows Every Hair Reads As Digital.
- Leave A Thin Rim Of The Original Background Or Of White Paper Around The Subject, A Few Pixels Wide On A 1080-Wide Frame.
- Mix Edge Types With Intent. Clean Facets For Scissors. A Rough, Fibrous Edge Drawn With Seeded Noise For A Tear.
- Give Every Piece A Short, Soft Shadow In One Shared Direction. The Shadow Is What Separates Paper From Paper.

## One Treatment For Every Photo

- Convert Photos To A Single Tone Or To Two Tones Mapped To The Palette, And Raise The Contrast.
- Apply One Print Texture To All Of Them, Such As Halftone Dots Or Coarse Grain. Size The Texture To The Output Frame, Not To Each Source, So A Small Photo And A Large One Share The Same Dot.
- Full-Color Photos From Different Sources, Left As They Are, Produce A Scrapbook.

## Paper And Extras

- Draw The Ground As Paper With Visible Grain. Give Colored Blocks A Slight Variation In Tone.
- Tape, Staples, Pen Circles, Arrows, Stamps, And Ruled Lines Each Need A Job, Such As Pointing Or Labeling. Use Few. Draw Pen Marks With The Line Rules In [Hand-Made 2D](handmade-2d.md).

## Type

- Type Is Half The Image. Pair One Bold Display Face With One Plain Text Face, Both Installed And Licensed.
- Set Type Large, Tight, And On The Grid. Let It Run Behind And In Front Of Cut-Outs.
- Put Words On Paper Blocks Or Strips When The Photo Behind Them Is Busy.
- Make Figures Very Large. In This Style A Number Is A Picture.
- Hold Every Line Long Enough To Read At Phone Size.

## Color And Light

- Use Two Or Three Colors, Black, And The Paper. Give One Accent A Fixed Meaning From [Directing](../DIRECTING.md).
- Keep Fills Flat. Let Tone Come From The Print Texture.
- There Is No Computed Light. Depth Comes From The Shadows Between Layers, So Keep Their Direction And Length The Same Everywhere.

## Movement And Frame Rate

- Step The Movement. Hold Each Position For Two Or Three Frames, As In Stop Motion.
- Add A Small Seeded Error To Each Held Position, About A Pixel Of Shift And A Fraction Of A Degree Of Turn, So Pieces Look Placed By Hand.
- Bring Pieces In With A Short Slide Or Drop And A Slight Overshoot, Or Simply Cut Them In, On The Word That Names Them.
- Hinge Parts For Puppet Movement. A Cut-Out Arm Turns At A Pin And A Jaw Drops On A Hinge.
- Avoid Slow Eased Drifts Across A Photo. That Is A Slideshow.

## Camera

- The Camera Looks Down At A Table. Pan Across A Larger Layout, Cut Between Areas, And Push In A Little.
- Keep The Layers Almost Flat. They Are Paper, So Parallax Is Slight.
- Tilt Into Perspective Only When The Film Means To Show The Table Itself.

## Composition And Sequence

- Use Few Pieces Per Frame, Around Three To Five, With A Bold Difference In Scale. A Huge Head Beside A Tiny Building Is The Style.
- Let Pieces Overlap And Let The Frame Crop Them. Leave Bare Paper For The Eye To Rest On.
- Build Each Spread Piece By Piece In Step With The Voice. Each Arrival Is The Event For Its Beat, And The Finished Spread Is The Summary.
- Move Between Spreads With A Covering Block Of Paper, A Page Turn, Or One Piece That Carries Over.

## The Quality Bar

- Any Frame Could Be A Printed Page, With A Grid, A Hierarchy, And A Palette.
- Every Fragment Is Sourced, Cleared, And Treated To Match The Others.
- Edges Look Cut And Shadows Agree.
- Movement Looks Placed By Hand.
- Type Reads At Phone Size And Shapes The Composition.

## Typical Failures

| Failure | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| A Scrapbook | Limit The Palette, Give Every Photo The Same Treatment, And Snap To The Grid. |
| A Slideshow | Replace Eased Drifts With Stepped, Placed Movement. |
| Masks That Are Too Perfect | Rough The Cut And Leave A Rim. |
| Sources That Do Not Match | Unify Tone, Contrast, And Texture Size. |
| Shadows Pointing Different Ways | Set One Direction And Length In The Style Sheet. |
| Ornament On Every Corner | Keep Only Extras That Point Or Label. |
| Type Lost On A Photo | Put It On A Paper Block. |
| An Image That Is Not What The Words Claim | Replace It With The Real Thing Or Change The Words. |
