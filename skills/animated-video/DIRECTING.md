# Directing

Settle The Film In Words Before Any Scene Is Built. Words Are Cheap To Change And Scenes Are Not. This Guide Ends At An Approved Plan. The Look Is Chosen Next In [Style Frames](STYLE-FRAMES.md), The Build Follows [Code Scenes](CODE-SCENES.md) And [Voice Timing](VOICE-TIMING.md), And The Result Is Judged With [Review](REVIEW.md).

## The Order Of Work

This Guide Covers Steps One And Three Of The Workflow In The [Skill Guide](SKILL.md). Each Part Needs Only What Came Before It.

1. **The Brief.** Run The Intake Below And Have The Format Confirmed. This Needs Nothing But The Owner's Request.
2. **The Film Folder.** Create The Film Folder In The User's Project With The Script And Its Word Timings, As [Voice Timing](VOICE-TIMING.md) Describes. With No Recording Yet, These Are Stand-In Timings. With No Script Yet, Draft One From The Brief And Show It With The Plan.
3. **The Plan.** Find The Pictures, Write The Timed Beat Sheet From Those Word Timings, Render Rough Direction Stills Where They Help, And Stop For Approval.

**What May Be Drawn Before Approval.** Before The Plan Is Approved, Draw Only Rough Direction Stills: Plain Shapes And Flat Fills In One Neutral Treatment, For The Two Or Three Beats That Carry The Film. Keep Them In A `directions` Folder Inside The Film Folder So They Are Not Mistaken For The Film. Before The Look Is Approved, Add Only The Style Frames From [Style Frames](STYLE-FRAMES.md). No Finished Scene Is Built Before Both Approvals: No Motion Polish, No Texture Pass, No Caption Styling, And No Sound.

## Principles

- **Push The Idea As Far As It Will Go.** Judge Every Idea By Whether The Viewer Understands Faster Or Remembers Longer. A Plain Picture That Explains At Once Beats A Clever One That Must Be Decoded. When Two Pictures Explain Equally Well, Take The One Nobody Has Seen Before.
- **Pictures Explain, Narration Agrees.** Muted, The Pictures Should Still Carry The Claims. The Voice Names What The Eye Has Just Understood. When The Voice Does The Explaining And The Picture Only Accompanies It, The Picture Is Wallpaper.
- **Decide Early What Each Color Means, And Keep It.** One Color For The Thing The Viewer Follows, One For The Problem, Neutrals For Everything Else. Once A Color Has A Meaning, It Is Never Used As Decoration. A Change Of Color Then Becomes An Event The Viewer Can Read.
- **Design Fresh For Each Brief.** Past Films And The Reusable [Parts](PARTS.md) Are Head Starts. Use A Part When It Is The Right Picture For This Claim, Not Because It Already Exists.
- **Record What Was Wrong With A Rejected Idea.** When The Owner Rejects A Talking Jar As Too Childish, That Is A Note About Tone. It Does Not Ban Characters. Write Down The Fault In The Owner's Words And Leave The Rest Of The Category Open.
- **Ask With A Recommendation.** Put Your Preferred Answer Beside Every Question So That Yes Is A Complete Reply.

## Story Or Explainer

| | Story | Explainer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What It Is | Someone Who Wants Something, Meets An Obstacle, And Changes | How Or Why Something Works |
| The Viewer Leaves With | A Feeling Attached To A Decision Or A Value | An Understanding They Can Repeat To Someone Else |
| Its Shape | A Want, An Obstacle, A Choice, A Changed State | A Question, A Mechanism In Steps, A Consequence |
| Choose It When | The Point Is A Change In A Person, A Reason For Existing, Or A Before And After | The Point Is A Mechanism, A Process, A Comparison, Or A Quantity |
| It Must Open With | The Want | The Question |

Test The Brief In One Sentence. If It Reads As Someone Wants Something But Something Is In The Way, It Is A Story. If It Reads As This Happens Because Of That, It Is An Explainer. When A Brief Holds Both, Choose One As The Spine And Let The Other Visit. An Explainer Forced Into A Story Gains A Mascot Who Wants Nothing And Delays The Explanation. A Story Forced Into An Explainer States Feelings As Bullet Points.

## Intake

Ask Everything In One Message, Each Question With A Recommendation. Read What The Owner Already Supplied First And Ask Only What Is Missing.

| Ask | It Decides | A Recommendation To Offer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What Is The One Sentence The Viewer Should Remember? | What Every Beat Is Tested Against | Your Own Draft Of The Sentence |
| Who Is Watching, And What Do They Already Know? | Where The Explanation May Begin | Your Reading Of The Audience From The Material |
| Where Will It Be Shown? | The Aspect Ratio And The Length | The Frame And Length That Suit The Destination |
| Story Or Explainer? | The Shape Of The Film | Your Reading Of The Brief, With The Reason |
| Is There A Recorded Voice, Will One Be Recorded, Or Will The Film Have No Narration? | Where The Timing Comes From, And Whether The Film Can Be Exported Before A Recording Exists | Stand-In Timings Now, Retimed When A Recording Arrives. A Film With No Narration Says So In Its Storyboard, As [Voice Timing](VOICE-TIMING.md) Describes |
| Which Films Do You Like, And What Do You Like About Each? | Evidence Of Taste | Going Ahead Without A Reference When None Exists |
| What Real Material Exists? | What Must Appear, What Must Not, And Which Styles Are Possible | A List Of What You Found, With The Gaps Named |
| Will A Filmed [Presenter](PRESENTER.md) Appear? | Whether Frames Are Planned Around A Person From The Start | No Presenter, Unless The Film Depends On A Face |
| Which Music Or Sound? | Tone And Rights | The Kit's Own Sounds, Or A Track The Owner Has The Right To Use |

A Reference Is Studied For Timing And Structure, With Permission, And Never Lifted. Study Only What The Owner Supplies. The Kit Does Not Fetch Other People's Posts Or Media. Note Any Recommendation The Owner Did Not Answer As An Assumption, And Have It Confirmed At The Approval Stop.

**What The Owner Asked To See Decides How Much Is Shown.** When The Owner Asks Only To Confirm The Format And See One Style Frame, Do That. Confirm The Format, Keep The Plan Short, And Do Not Add Rounds They Did Not Ask For. The Plan Still Needs Their Yes, And For A Short Film It Can Travel In The Same Message As The Style Frame. See **The Approval Stop**.

## Find The Pictures

1. **List The Claims.** Split The Script Or The Idea Into Single Sentences. In An Explainer Each Is A Claim. In A Story Each Is A Moment.
2. **Study The References.** For Each Reference The Owner Supplied And Has Permission To Have Studied, Note How It Pictures Each Claim. Record What Is On Screen, Whether It Is Literal Or A Comparison, What Moves, And How Long It Holds. Keep Patterns, Not Assets. With No Reference, Go Straight To The Next Step. A Reference Is Useful Evidence, Not A Requirement.
3. **Brainstorm With A New Agent Kept Away From Production Limits.** A Fresh Agent Is A New Session That Has Seen Nothing From This Project Except What You Hand It. Give It The Claims, The Audience, And The One Sentence. Withhold The Tools, The Parts, The Style, And Past Films. An Agent That Knows The Limits Prunes Ideas Before They Exist. Ask For Several Different Kinds Of Picture For Every Claim Or Moment, Each Written As Something The Viewer Sees Happen.

| Kind | What It Is |
| --- | --- |
| Literal | The Thing Itself, Doing What The Claim Says |
| Everyday Analogy | A Familiar Object Or Situation With The Same Structure |
| Impossible Or Playful | Something Only Animation Can Show |
| Change Of Scale | Inside The Thing, The Whole System At Once, Or Time Compressed |
| Personified | The Thing As A Character With A Want |

4. **Pick With A Second Fresh Agent.** Give It The Brainstorm, The Brief, And A Short Account Of What The Kit Can Build. It Picks One Picture Per Beat, Weighing Clarity With The Sound Off, Surprise, The Visual Interest Of The Motion, And Build Cost. Clarity Comes First. A Picture That Fails With The Sound Off Is Out, However Surprising.
5. **Check The Picks As One Film.** Do The Pictures Belong To One World? Does One Object Recur And Transform, Or Does Every Beat Start A New Metaphor? Can The End Of One Picture Become The Start Of The Next? Is The Most Surprising Picture On The Most Important Beat? Does The Total Build Cost Fit? Do The Color Meanings Hold?

When No Fresh Agent Can Be Started, Run The Two Passes Yourself As Separate Steps And Say So In The Report.

## Let The Owner Choose From Rendered Directions

People Agree To Descriptions And Then Reject The Pictures. For The Two Or Three Beats That Carry The Film, Usually The Opening, The Central Mechanism Or Turn, And The Ending, Render Two Or Three Directions Each As Stills At The Delivery Aspect Ratio. Use `tools/stills.cjs` As Described In [Review Tools](REVIEW-TOOLS.md). Keep These In One Plain Treatment So The Owner Chooses The Idea And Not The Finish. Stills Are Not Video Files, So They Can Be Shown Before Any Approval.

These Are The Rough Direction Stills, And They Are All That May Be Drawn Before The Plan Is Approved. Each One Is A Throwaway Scene Module That Places Plain Shapes For A Single Frame. It Needs The Film Folder And The Word Timings From The Step Before, And Nothing Else. When The Owner Asked For Less, Show Less: Render The One Direction You Recommend For Each Key Beat, Name The Alternatives In A Line, And Offer To Render Them.

## Pitch Concepts That Differ

Assemble Two Or Three Concepts For The Whole Film From The Picks. They Must Differ In Kind. Change The Central Picture, The Structure, Or The Register. The Same Idea In Three Palettes Is One Concept.

- Give Each Concept A Name, Its Central Picture In One Sentence, How It Opens, How It Ends, Its Build Cost, And Its Main Risk.
- Show Each Concept Through Its Rendered Key Beats. The Stills Are What The Owner Chooses From.
- Test The Difference. A Viewer Should Be Able To Tell The Concepts Apart From A Single Frame.
- Recommend One And Say Why.

## The Timed Beat Sheet

Write One Line Per Beat. Each Line Needs An Event On Screen. An Event Is Something The Viewer Could Describe With A Verb. A Jar Is A Still. A Jar That Fills And Overflows Is An Event. A Line With No Event Is Merged, Cut, Or Sent Back For A Picture.

| Seconds | Voice | Picture Event | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.0 To 2.4 | Your Headphones Do Not Block Noise. | A Wall Slides Between The Street And The Ear, Then Cracks And Falls Away. | A Low Hit On The Crack |
| 2.4 To 5.8 | They Listen To It. | A Small Microphone On The Ear Cup Opens Like An Eye And Follows The Incoming Wave. | A Soft Tick As It Opens |
| 5.8 To 9.5 | Then They Play The Exact Opposite. | A Second Wave Leaves The Speaker, Mirrored, In The Second Color. | A Rising Sweep |
| 9.5 To 12.0 | Peak Meets Trough, And Both Go Flat. | The Two Waves Overlap And Collapse Into A Level Line That Reaches The Ear. | The Bed Drops Away |

Take The Times From The Word Timings In The Film Folder. With A Recording, They Are Its Word Timings. Before One Exists, They Are Stand-In Timings From `tools/standin-words.cjs`, Marked As Such On The Beat Sheet, And The Film Is Retimed With `tools/retime.cjs` When The Recording Arrives. For A Film With No Narration, They Are The Reading Pace Or The Times The Author Chose. [Voice Timing](VOICE-TIMING.md) Has The Method. Each Event Lands On, Or Just Before, The Word That Names It.

## Checks Before Showing It

**For An Explainer**

- Is One Question Posed In The Opening Seconds And Answered By The End?
- Can Each Step Be Understood From Its Picture With The Voice Off?
- Does Each Step Rely Only On What Has Already Been Shown?
- Is The Mechanism Shown Working, Or Only Labeled? Arrows Pointing At Parts Are A Diagram, Not An Explanation.
- Is There A Moment That Shows What Happens Without It?
- Are Quantities Shown As Lengths, Counts, Or Areas The Eye Can Compare?
- Is Every Figure Traceable To A Source The Owner Supplied?

**For A Story**

- Who Wants What, And Is It Shown By An Action In The Opening Seconds?
- Is The Obstacle Visible?
- Does The Character Cause The Change By Choosing Or Doing Something?
- Does The Last Picture Show The Change When Set Beside The First?
- Could Any Scene Be Removed Without Changing The Ending? Remove It.
- Does The Owner's Point Arrive As Part Of The Resolution, Or As A Logo Added Afterward?

**For Both**

- Does The First Frame Give A Reason To Keep Watching?
- Does The Last Picture Land The One Sentence?
- Does Each Beat Carry One New Idea?

## The Approval Stop

Show The Concepts, The Rendered Key Beats, And The Beat Sheet For The Concept You Recommend, Together With Your Assumptions And Any Open Questions. One Message Lets A Yes Approve The Whole Plan.

- Wait For An Explicit Answer. Silence Is Not Approval. Interest Is Not Approval.
- Do Not Build Scenes While You Wait.
- Approval Of The Plan Is Not Approval Of The Look, And Neither Is Approval To Export. Each Has Its Own Stop.
- **One Message For A Short Film.** The Plan Stop And The Look Stop May Share One Message When The Film Runs About A Minute Or Less And Has One Clear Concept, Or When The Owner Asked To See A Style Frame Straight Away. Put The Format, The Beat Sheet, And The Recommended Style Frame In That Message, And Ask Two Separate Questions, One For The Plan And One For The Look. Each Stop Still Needs Its Own Explicit Yes. A Reply That Clearly Covers Both, Such As Yes To Both, Approves Both. A Reply About Only One Leaves The Other Open, So Ask Again Before Building.
- Keep The Two Stops Apart For A Longer Film, For Concepts That Compete, And Whenever The Look Depends On Which Concept Wins.
- Record What Was Approved, In The Owner's Words. If Something Was Rejected, Record What Was Wrong With It And What They Liked, And Let That Reason Steer The Next Round.
